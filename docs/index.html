Attention Explorer

A 0.8M-parameter character-level GPT trained on Shakespeare. Pick a character and see which earlier characters each attention head reads from when predicting what comes next. The model runs in your browser, so you can also switch heads off and watch what breaks.

The 16 heads

Each square is one head's attention on your text: row = the character doing the looking, column = the character looked at. Click a head to trace it above. Flip the switch to turn it off; everything on the page, including generation, uses the model with that head removed.

Make it write

The model continues your text one character at a time, using whichever heads are switched on. Try it with every head on, then with the previous-character heads off.

Related work

None of these techniques is new. This project reproduces known results on a model small enough to run in a web page.

  • Michel, Levy & Neubig 2019, Are Sixteen Heads Really Better than One? Most heads can be removed one at a time with little loss. The single-head ablations here agree.
  • Voita et al. 2019, Analyzing Multi-Head Self-Attention: Specialized Heads Do the Heavy Lifting. Found positional heads attending to adjacent tokens, like layer 0's previous-character heads.
  • Elhage et al. 2021, A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits, and Olsson et al. 2022, In-context Learning and Induction Heads. Previous-token heads are the first half of an induction head.
  • nostalgebraist 2020, interpreting GPT: the logit lens, and Belrose et al. 2023, the tuned lens. The layer-by-layer decoding used above.
  • Vig 2019, BertViz, and TransformerLens. The standard tools for viewing attention and running these experiments on real models.
  • Karpathy 2023, Let's build GPT. The architecture and training setup.
